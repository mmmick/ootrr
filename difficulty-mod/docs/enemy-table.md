# Enemy table

A reference of every fighting enemy in the game files: hit points, damage, whether the speed setting reaches it, backup role, home maps and where it cannot go. Read on 2026-11-19 from the extracted game files (actor packs, life, attack, movement and attack-pace parameters, map placement files, AI graphs) with a throwaway reader on `playtest/next`; raw rows are in `enemy-table.csv`.

**How to read it.** HP is the game's life number (100 means the fight is scripted). Damage is in quarter hearts: base attack power times each attack's multiplier, ignoring zero entries (a Deku Baba bite works out to 2 and was seen as 2). Attack names are not stored in the files, only a count. "?" means the files do not say. "Speed" says whether the speed setting changes the enemy and whether it moves by a speed number (a guess from its AI graph: if the graph moves it with animation, the number matters less). Nothing in the files literally says "flat ground only": the Ground note gives the evidence found (path finding, navmesh, cliff and slope checks). Variants (always-respawn, "Truth", fixed, stage copies) are grouped on one row; Haribote dummies, attention markers, photo performers, leaf props and the Keese "Truth" copy are left out.

## Table 2: backup

**Current rules** (`backup.rs`, setting `enemy_backup`). An enemy that runs the shared enemy graph and is not on the never-call list can call for help once, only while it has a valid target (it is in a fight). A timer of 600 frames plus a random 0 to 900 frames (10 to 25 seconds at 60 frames a second) runs; when it ends one helper is woken 2.5 units to the caller's side. One helper at a time, one call per enemy for life (a latch is set, so it never calls again, even after the helper dies). The helper is a normal enemy actor created from a holder entry in the caller's pack, so it appears even in maps that never placed it, and it must not run the shared graph (so it cannot call in turn).

**Who calls whom: three options to choose from.** **Chosen on 19 Nov: option 3, as written** (built on `feature/backup-r3`, not yet seen in the game). Club Moblin falls back to its option 2 helper (one Guay), because the spear Moblin did not work when created. A called helper is marked when it is woken and never calls in turn.

- **Option 1: small fry by theme.** What the game does now, with one fix: Fire Keese did not appear when created away from home, so its callers get a plain Keese. Every helper has 1 or 2 health and can never call anyone. Safest; all helpers were seen appearing.
- **Option 2: a smaller relative.** The helper is a weaker cousin of the caller where one exists (Stalchild for the skeleton family, Biri for the Bari, wall Skulltula for forest enemies), otherwise as option 1. Slightly harder. Stalchildren and Biri would be new as helpers: Stalchildren appeared when created; a Biri appeared but only drifted.
- **Option 3: bring a friend.** The helper is the same kind or a peer, so every fight can double once. Much harder. Needs one code change first: a helper created this way must be barred from calling in turn (today only whole kinds are barred), and each new helper kind must be seen appearing. ## What the numbers suggest

- **Speed setting.** 25 ordinary enemy rows have no movement-speed file at all, so the speed setting does nothing to their walking: Amos (Armos statue), Anubis (flame statue), Beamos, Bubble, green, Bubble, red, Business Scrub, Crazy floor tile, Deku Baba, Deku Scrub (shooting), Flying pot, Forest scrubs (Lost Woods stage), Gohma egg, Mad Scrub, Moblin, club, Octorok, Peahat, Peahat larva, Shabom (bubble), Shell Blade, Skulltula, Skulltula, gold, Skullwalltula, Tentacle (Jabu-Jabu), Tricky door, Wallmaster. Of those with the file, the ones whose AI graph does not use animation-driven movement (Wolfos, Keese, Lizalfos, Poe, Gibdo, ReDead, little Floormaster...) should react fully; Stalfos, Moblin, Tektite and Dodongo move partly by animation. This matches the seen result that only the Wolfos was dramatically faster. The "attack pace" half of the setting only reaches enemies that have their own attack-pace file (marked "attack pace scaled").
- **Health.** Most small enemies have 1 to 3 hp, Stalfos and Dinolfos 18, Iron Knuckle 34, bosses 15 to 70. Values of 100 or more are scripted (scrubs, Morpha tentacle) and the health setting leaves them alone. All five hp values seen on the overlay match the files.
- **Damage setting gaps.** Grabbers have base power 0 so their grab is not in the attack file and the damage setting does not touch it: Dead Hand and hands, Floormaster, little, Gibdo, ReDead, Wallmaster, BOSS Ganondorf, MINIBOSS Dark Link. Scrub nuts and a few projectiles live in other files.
- **Flat ground.** No file says "flat ground only". The enemies with the most ground machinery (path finding, navmesh, cliff and slope checks) are Stalfos, Tektites, Floormaster, Dinolfos, Gerudo fighters and Iron Knuckles: treat them as the pickiest. Fliers (Keese, Poes, Bubbles, Guay, Peahat) have no ground needs. Hangers and crawlers (Skulltula, Skullwalltula) are the least predictable off their home.
- **Unsafe away from home.** Seen: Fire Keese, Gibdo, blue Bubble, big Poe and a tool-made Silver Iron Knuckle did not appear; the spear Moblin does not work; Leevers need sand; Skulltulas hang. These are poor helpers or poor extras.
- **Backup default.** Most callers (all ground fighters not listed with a special helper) call a plain Keese. Fire Keese is the helper for Bubbles and Torch Slugs, and it did not appear in the spawn test, so those callers may call nobody: worth changing first.
- **Good and bad pairings.** Safe helpers that appeared: Keese, Ice Keese, Baby Dodongo, Guay, Flying pot, Shabom, Little Floormaster. Bad: Peahat larva (hurts enemies), Leever (needs sand), Fire Keese (did not appear). Pairing a Jabu-Jabu Shabom with a ground fighter is cheap and safe; a Baby Dodongo suits Lizalfos, Dinolfos and Dodongo, who already fight on ground.
- **Data gaps.** Attack names are not in the files; grab and projectile damage is missing for some enemies; "Placed in the game" counts only map placements, so enemies made by other actors (larvae, Stalchildren, little Floormasters) show none.
